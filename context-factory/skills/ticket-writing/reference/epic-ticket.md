# Epic ticket

Keep the Epic at outcome level. Put detailed requirements and acceptance criteria in self-contained child tickets.

Use the tracker's native parent-child relationship as the source of truth. Avoid a duplicate checklist that must be maintained separately. If native hierarchy is unavailable, use one maintained list of linked children.

```markdown
# [Epic] [Outcome-oriented title]

## Summary

[One or two short paragraphs explaining the need, affected users, why it matters, and overall change.]

## Target outcome

- [High-level capability or observable outcome.]
- [High-level capability or observable outcome.]

## Scope

### Included

- [Major journey, area, or capability.]

### Not included

- [Important adjacent work intentionally excluded.]

## Related issues

[Group the child implementation, bug, and spike tickets using the tracker's native hierarchy.]
```

Add detailed business rules, technical design, dependencies, or ticket-level acceptance criteria only when essential to explaining the Epic's boundary. Keep the full details in the relevant child tickets.
