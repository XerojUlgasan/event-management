# Unslop

Follow the [Clemora workflow adapters](../../shared/skill-adapters.md) and [operating contract](../../shared/agent-workflow.md).

Read the whole passage, identify its audience and purpose, and preserve the intended meaning, source attribution, terminology, and necessary confidence language.

Remove patterns that make a reader work without adding information:

- Vague attributions and unsupported claims. Name the source or remove the claim.
- Inflated vocabulary, metaphor nouns, fancy substitutes for is/has, and verbs that describe a feeling instead of a mechanism.
- Stock openings/closers, flattery, filler, repeated previews, unnecessary hedging, and generic conclusions.
- Forced contrasts, artificial groups of three, meaningless ranges, and synonym cycling for one concept.
- Dense noun chains, overcompressed fragments, ambiguous pronouns, passive clauses hiding the actor, and adverbs replacing a specific fact.
- Decorative headings/emojis, excessive bold labels, repeated inline labels, and punctuation used to avoid writing a clear sentence.

Use familiar words, concrete subjects, active verbs, and varied sentence lengths. State the mechanism or observed result. Keep relevant technical names accurate and keep each concept's name consistent. Use formatting when it aids comparison or navigation, rather than banning useful lists or headings categorically.

For Clemora, “the API saves the inquiry and linked conversation in a transaction” is more useful than claiming the flow is seamless. Do not remove an evidence limit merely to sound decisive. [Why](../why/SKILL.md) confidence terms, citation qualifiers, and the distinction between intended, implemented, and observed behavior are substantive.

For durable documents, pair this pass with [technical writing](../technical-writing/SKILL.md) and the documentation agent. Wording edits alone do not establish technical correctness.
